# Growth kit: PROPOSAL

Written by the growth role on 2026-10-02. **This is a proposal.** The role file places the kit at `~/videos/lib/docs/growth-kit.md`; `~/videos/lib` is a git repository that I do not change. If the coordinator and the owner accept it, someone with the owner's word copies it there and commits. Until then nothing in it binds a producer.

It holds four things: the wordings for each kind of ask (English and Brazilian Portuguese), the end-screen template, the link block, and the placement rules as a checklist.

What stands behind each part is labelled: **[Platform]** (quoted in `growth/PLATFORM-RULES.md`), **[Research]**, **[Practice]**, **[Judgement]**. We have no analytics of our own yet, so every wording here is a hypothesis.

---

## 1. Principles the wordings follow

1. The film's ending finishes first: last line, last sound, then the ask.
2. One primary ask per piece. In a long film it is the next film.
3. An ask that is on screen is spoken, in the film's own voice.
4. Say what the viewer gets. Never "don't forget to like and subscribe", never "smash", never a countdown, never "before you go".
5. Promise nothing the owner has not confirmed. **No wording below mentions a schedule** ("every week", "next Tuesday"): the publishing frequency is unknown. When the owner gives one, the marked variants may be used.
6. Nothing is offered in exchange for a subscription, a like or a comment. **[Platform]** asking is allowed; rewards are not.
7. A named film is named by its title exactly as published, and it must be public when the film that names it goes out.

**[Research]** The one study found (Anderson, Nguyen and Anderson 2026, abstract only) reports no significant association between a spoken "subscribe" prompt and subscriptions across 8,500 videos. So the kit spends its one spoken sentence on the next film, and leaves subscribing to YouTube's own element.

## 2. Wordings

Square brackets are slots. `[TITLE]` is the next film's published title. `[LINK]` is one clause, from this film's own content, saying why that film follows.

### 2.1 Next film, spoken in the end-screen section (the default ask)

| | English | Português (Brasil) |
|---|---|---|
| Pattern | "[LINK] is the next film: [TITLE]." | "[LINK] é o próximo filme: [TITLE]." |
| Example (v021 to v024) | "The clocks that theory keeps right are in the next film: Your phone needs Einstein to find you." | "Os relógios que essa teoria mantém certos estão no próximo filme: Seu celular precisa de Einstein para achar você." |
| When the next thing is a playlist | "The films on screen now [LINK]." | "Os filmes na tela agora [LINK]." |

Rules for `[LINK]`: it restates something this film has already said, in a few words; it makes no new claim (a new claim needs a ledger row and the researcher); it does not summarise the next film's answer.

The Portuguese title in the example is illustrative only: there is no published Portuguese title for v024, and rule 7 applies in each language.

### 2.2 Mid-film pointer (at most one per film, and only when the narration already mentions the other film's subject)

No spoken words are added: the narration's own sentence is the reason, and a card carries the link. Card teaser text, eight words or fewer:

| | English | Português (Brasil) |
|---|---|---|
| Pattern | "[the subject, as the other film frames it]" | idem |
| Example (v024 at 3:27 to v021) | "The 1919 eclipse, in Sobral" | "O eclipse de 1919, em Sobral" |
| Example (v028 at 3:27 to v029) | "Another filter: the missing bullet holes" | "Outro filtro: os furos de bala que faltam" |

**[Platform]** a misleadingly labelled card is named in the Fake engagement policy; the teaser says what the other film is, nothing more.

### 2.3 Subscribe, spoken (only when a film has no next film to offer, or when the owner asks for it)

| | English | Português (Brasil) |
|---|---|---|
| Without a schedule (usable today) | "If you want the next idea worked through like this one, subscribing is how it reaches you." | "Se você quer a próxima ideia explicada assim, é se inscrevendo que ela chega até você." |
| With a schedule (**only after the owner confirms it**) | "One idea like this, explained properly, [every week]. Subscribing is how it reaches you." | "Uma ideia como esta, explicada direito, [toda semana]. É se inscrevendo que ela chega até você." |

Not used in the same section as 2.1. If both the next film and Subscribe elements are on screen, only the next film is spoken; the Subscribe circle is YouTube's own control and carries no text of ours.

### 2.4 The bell

Not asked for anywhere. **[Platform]** a subscriber already gets "highlights"; the bell changes that to every upload. **[Judgement]** asking for it is a second ask stacked on a first, for a channel whose frequency is unknown. Revisit when there is a schedule.

### 2.5 A Short's closing line (only when a long film of ours answers the question the Short leaves open)

| | English | Português (Brasil) |
|---|---|---|
| Pattern | "[What is left open] is a longer story." | "[O que ficou em aberto] é uma história mais longa." |
| Example (S04 to v020) | "Why it turns so slowly is a longer story." | "Por que ele gira tão devagar é uma história mais longa." |
| Variant that names the film | "[What is left open] is a longer story: [TITLE]." | "... é uma história mais longa: [TITLE]." |

One line, in the last three seconds, after the payoff. The long film must really answer what the line leaves open. A Short that leads nowhere ends on its own last line and asks for nothing (S07, S10). A Short never says "watch the rest" or "full video on the channel": it is not part of another film.

### 2.6 A comment prompt (only when the film itself hands the viewer a question)

The film's own closing question is the prompt (S18: "How many pairs are in the room you are in?"). Nothing is added to the film. The pinned comment gives the means to answer and one line of invitation:

| | English | Português (Brasil) |
|---|---|---|
| Pattern | "[how to work it out]. Tell us [yours]." | "[como calcular]. Conte para a gente [o seu]." |

### 2.7 The pinned comment

One per film, written for that film, never the same text twice (**[Platform]** repeated identical comments are the comment-spam example). It does one of three jobs, in this order of preference:

1. **States the film's most important limit in plain words** (v025: the map is our simulation; v024: eleven kilometres is not what your dot would do; v029: the exchange is a legend).
2. **Gives the viewer something to try** (S10: a second sum; S18: the pairs formula).
3. **Answers the question most likely to be asked.**

It asks for nothing. On a long film it may end "Sources are in the description." On a Short it carries no link (**[Platform]** not clickable).

### 2.8 Description: the first lines

The first paragraph is the film's question and the fact that makes it bite, written so it stands alone when cut off before "more". The publishing editor's descriptions already do this; the kit adds one line, placed after the opening paragraphs and before the first block in capitals:

| | English | Português (Brasil) |
|---|---|---|
| Next line | "Next: [TITLE]: [link]" | "Em seguida: [TITLE]: [link]" |

Only when that film is public. Removed or changed when a better next film appears.

## 3. The end-screen template

One section at the end of every long film. It replaces the separate credits card. Built once in the brand's look by the art director; each film fills three slots (next film's title, credits, backdrop).

### 3.1 Platform limits it is built to **[Platform]**

- The end screen may occupy only "the last 5–20 seconds".
- Up to four elements on 16:9; we use two.
- Watermark and card teasers are suppressed during it.
- It may not show at all on some devices, and viewers can hide it. So **the section must read as a complete ending with every YouTube element absent.**

### 3.2 Length and timeline

Section length **16.0 s**. `T` is the section's first frame. The section begins no sooner than 1.5 s after the film's last spoken word ends, and after the film's closing picture has been held.

| Time | Picture | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| T−1.5 to T | The film's closing picture, held. Nothing of ours is added | The film's own ending resolves |
| T to T+1.0 | The closing picture gives way to the layout (a move or a dissolve, not a cut to black) | Music re-enters or continues, quieter |
| T+1.0 to about T+6.0 | Layout complete. Next film's title appears under the window as it is spoken | The spoken line (2.1), once |
| T+6.0 to T+14.5 | Static. Credits readable. Nothing moves in the two clear zones | Music alone |
| T+14.5 to T+16.0 | Hold, then end. No fade to black before the last frame | The music's last note has ended by T+15.5 |

YouTube elements, set in Studio by the owner: Video (or Playlist) from T+1.0 to the end; Subscribe from T+6.0 to the end. **[Platform]** elements may have different start times. **[Judgement]** the film first, then the button, so the two do not compete during the spoken line.

**[Judgement] on 16 s:** the style guide records a reference film whose 17-second end card is guessed to be where viewers leave, and its own tip is "end soon after the payoff". Sixteen seconds is the shortest that lets a spoken sentence finish and still leaves a viewer ten seconds to act. It is the first number to revisit when analytics arrive: if viewers leave at T, try 12 s.

### 3.3 Layout, 1920×1080 (x from the left, y from the top)

| Zone | Rectangle | What is there in our picture |
|---|---|---|
| **N** next-film window | x 96–864, y 168–600 (768×432, 16:9) | A panel in `pn-ink-raised` (#22252C) with a 2 px `pn-rule` (#3A3E47) outline. **Otherwise empty.** YouTube's Video element is dragged to cover it |
| Kicker | x 96–864, y 108–144 | "NEXT" (Portuguese: "A SEGUIR"), Archivo, capitals, 36 px, `pn-cream-muted` (#B8B1A4). Counts as a label of the window |
| Next film's title | x 96–864, y 624–700, up to two lines | Archivo display, 44–48 px, `pn-cream` (#F2EBDD). Appears as the narrator says it |
| **S** subscribe circle | x 96–296, y 716–916 (200×200) | **Empty**: ink only, no ring, no word. YouTube's Subscribe element is dragged here |
| **C** credits column | x 960–1824, y 168–960 (864×792) | Heading "SOURCES AND CREDITS" 36 px `pn-cream-muted`; body Archivo 36 px, 54 px leading, `pn-cream`; up to 13 lines of about 46 characters. Licence credits (CC BY and others) complete |
| Notch | x 0–64, y 1016–1080 | The brand notch, `pn-orange` (#E8581C), as on every card |
| Backdrop | everything else | `pn-ink` (#16181D), or the film's home-base picture dimmed to no more than a quarter of its brightness, never under N, S or C |
| Margins | 96 px left and right; nothing of ours below y 960 except the notch | |

Why this shape:

- **N on the left, large.** It is the primary ask; a 768×432 window is more than twice the area of the largest box that fits beside today's credits cards (see `RETROFIT-published.md`: 356×200 on v024 and v025).
- **C on the right, at 36 px.** The art director's standing rule is 36 px minimum for text; today's credits cards are 24–28 px and six films have been flagged for it. The column holds about 600 characters: v025's nine credit lines (about 520 characters) fit; v024's seven long lines need re-breaking, not cutting.
- **S small and wordless.** It is the secondary ask and it is the platform's control, not our picture. No arrow, no "subscribe" lettering, no animation pointing at it.
- **Nothing moves** in N or S, and nothing of ours is ever under them.

For the art director to decide: whether N's panel shows a still from the next film when YouTube's element is absent. **[Judgement]** I would leave it empty with the title beneath: a still that the element then covers with its own thumbnail would flicker between two pictures, and a still of another film is an asset to keep in step with that film's final cut.

### 3.4 Sound **[the owner's standing rule; the sound specialist's domain]**

- The film's own music, not a channel jingle: each film has its own sound design.
- Music runs under the whole section and ends on a resolved, low-register sound. No pure high tone, sweep or zap at the end; the tail is checked spectrally as for any film.
- The spoken line meets the same margin as any other line (`sr-margin`, floor 11 dB).
- No sound effect marks the appearance of N or S.

### 3.5 When there is no next film

Use a Playlist element in N ("All films", or the film's subject playlist) with the playlist wording in 2.1, or the Subscribe wording in 2.3 with N left as "best for viewer". Never name a film that is not public.

### 3.6 Vertical films (Shorts)

No section. A Short is not given an end card. Its ask, if it has one, is the closing line (2.5) and the related-video link set in Studio. End screens on Shorts could not be confirmed either way on YouTube Help.

## 4. The link block

Goes at the very end of every long film's description, after the credits. One block, same order everywhere, so a returning viewer knows where to look.

**English**

```
MORE FROM PALS'S NOTES
Subscribe: https://www.youtube.com/@PALSsNotes?sub_confirmation=1
More like this: [OWNER: link to this film's subject playlist]
[OWNER: one line per other page of the channel, "Name: link", most active first. Delete if none.]
[OWNER: "Corrections: address or page". Delete if none.]
```

**Português (Brasil)**

```
MAIS DO PALS'S NOTES
Inscreva-se: https://www.youtube.com/@PALSsNotes?sub_confirmation=1
Mais como este: [DONO: link da playlist do assunto deste filme]
[DONO: uma linha por outra página do canal, "Nome: link". Apagar se não houver.]
[DONO: "Correções: endereço ou página". Apagar se não houver.]
```

Notes:

- The channel address is the only link I know. Every other line is a placeholder for the owner; none is invented.
- `?sub_confirmation=1` is **[Practice]**: widely used, not documented on any Help page I found. Drop it if the owner wants only documented forms.
- **[Platform]** links in a long film's description are clickable only with advanced features. In a Short's description no URL is clickable; there the block is one line, the handle: `PALS's Notes: @PALSsNotes` (a mention, which is clickable).
- Up to three hashtags after the block, each a subject the film really covers.

## 5. Placement checklist for a producer

Run it when the screenplay is locked and again before the cut is reported. A "no" on a line marked ● stops the cut; the others are a note in `publish/GROWTH.md`.

**Before the ending is built**

- ● 1. The film has one primary ask, written down in `publish/GROWTH.md`: the next film (or playlist), and one sentence on why that one, quoting this film's own narration.
- ● 2. The next film is public, or will be before this one. If not: no spoken title; the element is "best for viewer".
- 3. If the narration mentions a subject another film of ours explains, the time is noted for a card (at most one mid-film pointer).
- ● 4. No ask of any kind in the first 30 seconds.

**The spoken line**

- ● 5. One sentence, in the film's voice, after the film's last line and after the payoff.
- ● 6. It names the next film by its published title, word for word.
- ● 7. It makes no new factual claim. If it does, it has a ledger row and the researcher has read it.
- ● 8. It promises no schedule, series or reward.
- 9. No "like", no "subscribe", no "bell", no "don't forget", no "before you go" in it.

**The section**

- ● 10. It starts at least 1.5 s after the last word, and the film's closing picture and sound have finished.
- ● 11. It is 16 s (between 12 and 20 is acceptable; over 20 the platform cannot cover it; under 12 the line and the viewer's time do not both fit).
- ● 12. Zones N (96–864, 168–600) and S (96–296, 716–916) contain nothing of ours but N's empty panel, on every frame of the section.
- ● 13. Every word on screen is either being spoken (the next film's title) or a label (NEXT, the credits heading) or a credit.
- ● 14. Credits are complete, at 36 px or more, inside C; every licence that asks for a credit on screen has it.
- ● 15. With the YouTube elements imagined absent, the frame still looks finished.
- 16. Music runs under the whole section; the last sound is low and resolved; `sr-audiocheck` and `sr-margin` pass with the line included.

**Around the film** (the publishing editor assembles; growth supplies the text)

- 17. Description: first paragraph stands alone; "Next:" line present only if that film is public; link block last; three hashtags at most.
- 18. Pinned comment written for this film; it states a limit, offers something to try, or answers the likely question; it asks for nothing.
- 19. Playlists named.
- 20. For Studio, in `publish/GROWTH.md`: element types, rectangles and start times (Video on N from T+1.0; Subscribe on S from T+6.0); the card's time, target and teaser.

**A Short**

- ● 21. No end card, no "watch the rest", no slice of a long film.
- ● 22. If it has a closing pointer, it is one line in the last three seconds, and the long film answers what the line leaves open.
- 23. Related video named in `publish/GROWTH.md`, to be set when that film is public. No URL in the description or pinned comment.

**After approval**

- ● 24. The rule-11 table (`qa/EDITORIAL.md`) has a row for the spoken line and a row for the static section, and the reviewer has checked both.
- 25. `publish/GROWTH.md` ends with what to measure: end-screen element click rate, card clicks, subscribers gained against views.

## 6. What this kit does not settle

- The owner's links, schedule and language decision (`growth/QUESTIONS-for-the-owner.md`).
- The smallest and largest element sizes Studio accepts: check on the first film that uses the template, with a private or unlisted upload, before the layout is frozen.
- Whether 16 s is right: first analytics.
- Whether the credits at 36 px fit every film's list: the art director, on v021.
