## 7. Two languages: one channel or two

**Status after the owner's answer (question 4, 2026-10-02):** the owner prefers English only, is located in Brazil, and asked for advice based on facts. The fact-based opinion is in **`ADVICE-language-location.md`** (Portuguese, with sources, access dates and a [F]/[P]/[N] label on each claim). The three-way analysis below was written before that answer and is kept as the description of the *Portuguese version* options, should the owner later want them.

The role says the channel publishes in English and Brazilian Portuguese. Today every film of the current batch is English only (v028's board entry: "versão em português não foi feita"), and several older films in `done/` are Portuguese. Three ways to do it. **The decision is the owner's; I give the trade-offs.**

### A. One channel, one video per film, Portuguese as a second audio track

**[Platform]** "The Multi-language audio feature allows you to upload audio tracks in different languages to a single video or Short... This is a powerful way to expand your global reach without creating and managing multiple separate channels." The track defaults to the viewer's language; translated titles and descriptions are searchable in that language; long films can also carry a translated thumbnail. Needs advanced features.

- For: one set of views, comments, subscribers and watch time per film; one end screen and one set of links to maintain; it is the route YouTube itself recommends.
- Against: **our films write on the screen.** Titles, labels, counters and the credits are burned into the picture in English; a Portuguese listener would hear Portuguese and read English. The channel's rule (anything on screen is what the narrator is saying or a label) breaks for that listener. The dub must be "roughly the same length" as the video, so the Portuguese narration has to fit English timings, and Portuguese usually runs longer.
- Cost per film: one narration, one mix, captions, translated title and description. No encode.

### B. One channel, two uploads per film, kept apart by playlists

- For: each language gets its own picture, timing, title, thumbnail and captions; one subscriber base; Portuguese playlists mirror the English ones.
- Against: every subscriber is offered every film twice; a visitor sees a page in two languages; views per film are split in two, and each half is what YouTube's recommendations see; end screens and "Next:" lines must stay inside one language, which doubles that work.
- Cost per film: a second narration, re-timed scenes, translated on-screen text, an encode, a review, a publishing package.

### C. Two channels

- For: each audience gets a page, a banner, an "about" and notifications in its own language; each film's numbers are clean; end screens and playlists never cross languages.
- Against: two channels each start from zero, and subscribers and watch hours are counted per channel toward any threshold (feature access, monetisation); two pages to keep; a second handle is needed and I do not know of one (I will not invent it); the production cost is the same as B.
- Cost per film: as B, plus the second channel's upkeep.

### What I can say without deciding

- **[Platform]** YouTube's own page argues for A.
- **[Judgement]** A is only as good as the films are free of on-screen language. Ours are not. A cheap test would settle how much that matters: add a Portuguese track, title, description and captions to one published film with little on-screen text, and after a few weeks look at Portuguese-track watch time and retention against the English track. v024 or v021 (when out) would be the film: the subject is Brazilian.
- **[Judgement]** B's main harm (a mixed feed) grows with every film; C's main harm (two cold starts) is paid once. If Portuguese versions will be made for most films, C ages better than B. If only a few, A or B.
- **Check now, whichever is chosen:** whether YouTube's automatic dubbing is already adding machine Portuguese to the published films (`PLATFORM-RULES.md`, section 13). **[Platform]** "You cannot edit automatic dubs."
