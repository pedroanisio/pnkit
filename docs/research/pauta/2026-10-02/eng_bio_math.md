## 1. The loose wire that dropped a bridge (Dali / Key Bridge)
- **It seems X, but really Y:** It seems a huge ship simply hit a weak bridge. Really, a ship blackout started with one signal wire not fully seated in a terminal block, because a heat-shrunk label band slid over the ferrule collar and made it too fat to insert; the rest was a chain (main engine trips on low cooling pressure, a "flushing pump" used as a fuel pump with no auto restart, breakers left in Manual) that stretched a 10 s recovery to 58 s.
- **Fact and source:** NTSB Marine Investigation Report MIR-25-40, adopted 2025-11-18. https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/AccidentReports/Reports/MIR2540.pdf . Read in part: abstract, table of contents, whole Executive Summary (downloaded, pdftotext). Quoted figures there: blackout 58 s vs 10 s in Automatic; bridge replacement estimated USD 4.3 to 5.2 billion, reopening late 2030; 34,000 vehicles a day displaced. Body chapters (timeline, pier protection analysis) not read.
- **Why now:** Not new in the window (report is 10 months old), but evergreen and there is no expiry. Rebuild news continues. Does not expire.
- **SHOW:** Animated single-line diagram of the electrical chain (HV bus, step-down transformer, LV bus, pumps) as a schematic we draw from the report's figures; a wire/ferrule/terminal-block cross-section diagram; timeline of 58 s vs 10 s with the ship's path on a map (map base: OpenStreetMap or NOAA chart, ODbL / US public domain). Physics of a 200,000-tonne ship's momentum: a simple simulation from real numbers in the report. NTSB report figures are US federal work (public domain), check each figure for third-party credit.
- **Format:** Long film (about 8 to 10 min); also Short: "the label band" (wire cross-section).
- **Overlap:** None with lists (Millennium Bridge, Braess are different). Close cousin to "why bridges have gaps" only by theme.
- **Risk:** Six workers died. Name no private person; no crew names; do not blame the crew beyond what NTSB says (NTSB lists the flushing-pump practice as not causal to the first blackout). Use "the report found". Low to medium.

## 2. "Renewables caused the blackout" - the Iberian blackout, 28 April 2025
- **It seems X, but really Y:** It seems a grid with too much solar and wind simply collapsed. Really, the expert panel found interacting causes: oscillations (0.63 Hz local, 0.2 Hz inter-area), gaps in voltage and reactive-power control, differing voltage-regulation practices and generators disconnecting as voltage rose; "It's not about renewables, it's about voltage control".
- **Fact and source:** ENTSO-E Expert Panel final report, published 2026-03-20. Page https://www.entsoe.eu/publications/blackout/28-april-2025-iberian-blackout/ (page read; PDF not opened, so the detailed sequence and numbers are NOT yet read; "read in part"). Quote about voltage control is from a news summary (lead only).
- **Why now:** 18 months after, the final report is out; public argument continues. Not expiring, but politically charged.
- **SHOW:** A real simulation we could run ourselves: a swing equation / coupled oscillator toy of grid frequency, and a voltage-rise cascade on a simple network, labelled as a model, not the event. Real data would come from the report's figures (check ENTSO-E reuse terms; unverified).
- **Format:** Long film candidate; Short on "grid inertia and oscillation".
- **Overlap:** Adjacent to GPS clocks / traffic jam only in spirit (cascades). None direct.
- **Risk:** Medium-high: energy politics. Rule: say only what the report says; do not take sides; read the full PDF before any claim. Needs the researcher to read the PDF.

## 3. Starliner: the thrusters that "overheated" (NASA investigation report)
- **It seems X, but really Y:** It seems the capsule's thrusters just got too hot. Really, the report's most probable direct causes: two-phase oxidizer flow (vaporization and cavitation), Teflon poppet extrusion in oxidizer valves restricting flow, and seals incompatible with the oxidizer (seven of eight helium manifolds leaked); root causes are qualification gaps and organisation. The report itself says the technical cause investigation continues and "iteration in proximate cause is expected".
- **Fact and source:** NASA, "Starliner Propulsion System Anomalies during the Crewed Flight Test - Investigation Report", signed 2026-02-05, compiled November 2025. https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/nasa-report-with-redactions-021926.pdf . Read in part: executive summary (downloaded, pdftotext). Body not read. A "NASA, Boeing Provide Starliner Update" item dated 2026-09-28 exists (lead only, not opened).
- **Why now:** Fresh update on 28 Sept (lead only); could change the cause story, so this one expires fast. Check before any script.
- **SHOW:** Diagram of a hypergolic thruster valve and poppet; two-phase flow explained with a small fluid cartoon; timeline of the 93-day mission (planned 8 to 14 days). NASA report figures are public (redacted version), credit NASA.
- **Format:** Long film or Short ("why liquid in a hot pipe boils").
- **Overlap:** None. Related to nothing on the lists.
- **Risk:** Medium: fast-moving, proximate cause may change, and the report is partly organisational and about named programs; avoid naming staff. Wait for the final technical cause.

## 6. Four colours, now in near-linear time (new computer proof, FOCS in November)
- **It seems X, but really Y:** It seems the four-colour theorem is old news (1976, 1996). Really, a 2026 proof shows that every planar triangulation contains linearly many pairwise non-touching reducible configurations at once, giving an O(n log n) four-colouring algorithm (previous best quadratic, 1996), by handling "flat" regions where every vertex has degree six. It still needs a computer.
- **Fact and sources:** arXiv:2603.24880 (v1 2026-03-25, v2 2026-05-07), Inoue, Kawarabayashi, Miyashita, Mohar, Thomassen, Thorup; abstract read on arXiv (abstract only). Quanta, 2026-09-10 (lead only, article read via fetch summary): 8,202 configurations, work began about 2015, FOCS presentation in November. FOCS date 2026-11-08 to 11, NYC (lead only).
- **Why now:** Quanta piece on 2026-09-10; conference 8 to 11 Nov gives a hook. Can run as a map film any time; the "why now" is soft.
- **SHOW:** Map colouring simulation: we run a real four-colouring (Kempe chain / greedy plus fix) on a real map (e.g. US states or EU countries, public domain borders) and a triangulation with a hexagonal flat patch; a real counting run showing "reducible configuration" example. All from our own code.
- **Format:** Long film; Short ("do maps really need only four colours? try it").
- **Overlap:** None on lists (coastline paradox, Mercator are maps but not this).
- **Risk:** Low. Careful: the near-linear claim is for the algorithm; "new proof" is computer-assisted.

## 7. Graham's rearrangement conjecture (1971) finished by four papers
- **It seems X, but really Y:** It seems any set of nonzero clock numbers must have some order that "breaks" by repeating a running total. Really, the conjecture says you can always order a set of nonzero residues mod a prime p so that all partial sums are distinct; it has now been shown for all sufficiently large primes via four papers (large sets 2022, small sets 2024, medium 2025, final range Feb 2026).
- **Fact and sources:** Pham and Sauermann, arXiv:2602.15797 (2026-02-17), abstract read (abstract only): proves it for subsets up to size p^(1-alpha), completing with earlier work "for all sufficiently large primes". Quanta 2026-09-28 (lead only via fetch summary) lists the chain arXiv:2204.09666, 2409.07403, 2508.18254. Not verified: the claim "complete resolution" for all primes (the abstract says sufficiently large p; small primes are presumably finite checks, unread).
- **Why now:** Quanta article 2026-09-28. No expiry.
- **SHOW:** A real brute-force run for small p (e.g. p = 7, 11, 13): for every subset, find a valid ordering and display the partial-sum walk on a clock face; count how many orderings work (grows fast). Real run, exact numbers.
- **Format:** Short (strongest), possibly long as juggling-style puzzle film.
- **Overlap:** Weak overlap with "matrix as a move"/"random walks (Polya)": none direct. New.
- **Risk:** Low. Keep "for sufficiently large primes" unless confirmed.
