- Fact + source: Pärssinen et al., "Over 20,000 precolonial earthworks in the Southwest Amazonia", Nature, published 2026-07-29, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10835-7 (DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10835-7). ### 2. The 1826 treaty that set the date, 200 years on (Brazil's slave-trade convention)
- It seems X: Brazil ended the Atlantic slave trade in 1850 because Britain forced it (Aberdeen Act). But really Y: Brazil had already signed, on 23 Nov 1826, a convention that made the trade illegal 3 years after ratification (piracy), as part of buying British recognition of independence; the 1831 law was the first domestic enforcement, then it collapsed; the 1850 law actually worked. The treaty's date and arrivals-per-year numbers make a clean "law on paper vs ships in the harbour" story.
- Fact + sources: Carta de Lei of 23 Nov 1826 ratifying the Convention, Coleção de Leis do Imperio, p.71, bilingual PT/EN, https://www2.camara.leg.br/legin/fed/carlei_sn/1824-1899/cartadelei-39883-23-novembro-1826-570862-anexo-pe.pdf . Read level: READ (I converted the PDF to text and read preamble, Art. I (three years after ratification, trade "deemed and treated as Piracy"), Art. IV (mixed commissions), Art. V; OCR is rough but legible). Ratified in London 13 Mar 1827 (secondary: Senado piece, below). Itamaraty archive record of the original ratification instrument dated 28 Feb 1827 (8 pp., digitised 34 MB PDF, no access restriction; photography allowed): https://atom.itamaraty.gov.br/index.php/p10-m31a-1827-02-28 (read in full, record page). Secondary read in full: Agencia Senado, https://www12.senado.leg.br/noticias/especiais/arquivo-s/marinha-britanica-forcou-brasil-a-acabar-com-trafico-negreiro : arrivals 51,000 (1830) to 6,000 (1831), 57,000 by 1837, ~73,000 peak in 1829, Aberdeen Act 1845, ~400 slave vessels captured 1845-1850, Eusebio de Queiros law Sept 1850, none by 1853. These numbers must be re-sourced to SlaveVoyages (slavevoyages.org) before use; SlaveVoyages dataset itself NOT opened by me (lead only: a 2026 History Compass article on the Indian Ocean extension exists).
- Why now: exact bicentenary 23 Nov 2026 (treaty date, read in the document). Expires after late Nov unless evergreen.
- SHOW: the treaty document images (Camara/Itamaraty, public domain Brazilian official documents; confirm), timeline bar of the 1826/1831/1845/1850 dates, a chart of annual arrivals from SlaveVoyages (real dataset, open licence to check), map of mixed-commission routes. No re-enactment.
- Format: long film (about 12 min) and a Short ("a 3-year countdown nobody kept").
- Overlap: none on the lists. Adjacent only to Sobral/Santos-Dumont by being Brazilian history.
- Risk: moderate. Subject is slavery and its legacy: sensitive, not partisan. Keep to documents and counted ships; avoid arguing about "who was to blame". Historians' views on British motives differ; present as documents + numbers.

### 3. The warm patch in the Pacific that rewires Brazil's rain (El Nino 2026-27, forecast "very strong")
- It seems X: floods in the South and drought and fires in the North are separate local disasters. But really Y: one warm anomaly in the equatorial Pacific (over 2 C in late September) shifts the atmosphere's circulation and tilts rain across the whole continent; the same event drives both.

INPE page https://www.gov.br/inpe/pt-br/assuntos/ultimas-noticias/painel-el-nino-2026-2027-quarto-boletim-sobre-o-monitoramento-do-fenomeno-no-brasil-e-publicado (read in full, but it is a news page; the bulletin PDF itself NOT opened; the 24/197 and >2 C numbers are from CNN Brasil https://www.cnnbrasil.com.br/nacional/brasil/el-nino-muito-forte-deve-trazer-seca-e-chuva-ao-brasil-ja-em-outubro/ read in full = lead level). Burn data: INPE Amazonas 2,384 hot spots 1-19 Sep 2026, +282% vs 2025, and ~20% of the 2024 record (lead only, regional news).

If any simulation is shown it must be a real run; the toy "Walker circulation" must be labelled as a diagram, not a simulation.
- Format: both. A Short can be timely ("why Brazil floods and burns at once"); the long film adds the history of the discovery (Walker, Peruvian fishermen: NOT researched by me).

### 4. Who named the photon? (centenary 18 Dec 2026)
- It seems X: Einstein named the photon, in 1905. But really Y: Einstein wrote "light quantum"; the word "photon" in physics comes from a G. N. Lewis letter to Nature on 18 Dec 1926, where it meant something else (a hypothetical "atom" that carries radiation and is conserved), and at least four people had used the word before for other purposes (Troland 1916 in vision science, Joly 1921, Wurmser 1925, Wolfers July 1926). Within a year physicists used Lewis's word for Einstein's quantum and dropped Lewis's theory.
- Fact + sources: Lewis, "The Conservation of Photons", Nature 118, 874-875, Dec 1926 (Crossref DOI 10.1038/118874a0; confirmed title/volume/pages only; the letter itself NOT opened: paywalled). Date 18 Dec 1926 and the quote "I therefore take the liberty of proposing for this hypothetical new atom, which is not light but plays an essential part in every process of radiation, the name photon." from APS News https://www.aps.org/publications/apsnews/201212/physicshistory.cfm (read in full; secondary). Priority claims: Kragh, "Photon: New light on an old name", https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.0293 (ABSTRACT only). New this month: Collavini et al., "From the Light Quantum to the Photon", arXiv 2609.04985, submitted 2026-09-04 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04985, abstract only).
- Why now: centenary 18 Dec 2026; fresh arXiv history paper (4 Sept 2026). Expires after Dec/early Jan.
- SHOW: the Nature page (1926: check public-domain status in US/UK before showing; show only a short quote if unclear), timeline strip 1905-1926-1927 (Solvay), our animation of a "conserved light atom" vs "energy packet" diagrams (concepts, not simulations), Compton-scattering geometry as a diagram.
- Format: Short (strong; one paradox) or short long-film chapter.
- Overlap: close to "GPS needs Einstein" and "time is an illusion" in theme (Einstein/light) but different subject (naming and a lost theory). No direct duplicate.
- Risk: low. A word-history film; keep the claim "Lewis popularised/coined it in physics" exact, with Kragh's priority caveat.

### 5. Route 66 was almost "Route 60": how a committee numbered America (centenary 11 Nov 2026)
- It seems X: the US highway numbers are historical accident and nostalgia. But really Y: a 1925 committee designed a grid (odd numbers north-south rising east to west, even numbers east-west rising north to south; main east-west routes end in 0) and the Chicago-Los Angeles road was assigned "60", broke the logic of Kentucky's governor, and ended up "66" because two state officials telegraphed "We prefer sixty six to sixty two".
- Fact + source: FHWA "From Names to Numbers", https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/infrastructure/numbers.cfm (read in full by fetch summary: Joint Board appointed 20 Feb 1925; numbering committee met 25 Sep 1925 in St Louis; 30 Apr 1926 telegram; Kentucky accepted July 1926; AASHO approved at Pinehurst NC, Nov 1926, ~96,626 miles after 132 requested changes). Exact day "11 Nov 1926" comes from a search summary of Wikipedia/other (lead only); FHWA page gives only "November 1926". Verify the day in AASHO minutes before narrating.
- Why now: centenary Nov 2026. Expires early 2027 (centennial of Route 66 runs through 2026).
- SHOW: map animation drawn from the numbering rules (our diagram), the 1925/26 maps (FHWA/Library of Congress, US government public domain), real numbers only.
- Format: Short (very good) and short long-film chapter in a "how systems get numbered" film.
- Overlap: none; tangential to "railway time" and "QWERTY" (Shorts about standards by committee): potential repetition of the "standard by committee" structure. Flag to coordinator, since the rule is no repeated structures across Shorts.
- Risk: low; US-centric, so weaker for the Portuguese audience.

### 6. "Equal in status": one sentence that turned an empire into a club (Balfour Report, Nov 1926)
- It seems X: the British Empire ended with decolonisation after 1945. But really Y: in Nov 1926 a conference committee defined Britain and the Dominions as "autonomous Communities within the British Empire, equal in status, in no way subordinate one to another in any aspect of their domestic or external affairs"; the Governor-General stopped being the London government's agent; the text became law in 1931 (Statute of Westminster; secondary).
- Fact + source: Report of the Inter-Imperial Relations Committee, Imperial Conference 1926, transcript https://www.foundingdocs.gov.au/resources/transcripts/cth11_doc_1926.pdf . Read level: READ the key passages (raw text; quote above checked verbatim; Governor-General section read). Document ends with date "November 18, 1926"; secondary sources (Britannica via search) say it was adopted 15 Nov 1926 and the conference opened 25 Oct 1926: reconcile before narrating.
- Why now: centenary mid-Nov 2026. Expires early 2027.
- SHOW: the typewritten report pages (Australian government transcript: check reuse terms), maps of dominions by date of self-government, diagram of the old vs new channel of communication (Governor-General vs High Commissioner).
- Format: Short, or a chapter in a longer "institutions" film.
- Overlap: none. 
- Risk: moderate: colonial history, but document-based and stable. Avoid reading it as a verdict on empire.

- Fact + source: "A new titanosauriform with European affinities in the Early Cretaceous of Brazil: insights on Somphospondyli phylogeny, histology and biogeography", Journal of Systematic Palaeontology 24(1), 2026 (Crossref DOI 10.1080/14772019.2025.2601579; online 2026-02-12; title read via Crossref, abstract NOT reached: publisher page 403). - SHOW: palaeogeographic maps (GPlates-style reconstruction using an open model such as Scotese/PALEOMAP: check licence), skeleton diagram from the paper; any "migration" animation must be labelled as a hypothesis, not a measured run.
