### 1. The "photon" was named by a man whose idea about it was wrong (centenary 18 Dec 2026)
- It seems: Einstein/Planck named the particle of light. But really: Gilbert Lewis coined "photon" in a Nature letter of 18 Dec 1926 for a different thing, an atom of light that is never created or destroyed. Einstein disagreed; physicists dropped his theory and kept his word (adopted by the 1927 Solvay conference, to which Lewis was not invited).
- Fact + source: SPIE Photonics Focus Sept/Oct 2026 "Naming the photon" https://spie.org/news/photonics-focus/septemberoctober-2026/naming-the-photon (abstract-level, summary read, 2026 article); APS News "December 18, 1926: Gilbert Lewis coins photon in letter to Nature" https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2012/12/gilbert-lewis-coins-photon-letter (lead only); Kragh, "Photon: new light on an old name" https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.0293 (lead only, to be read as primary history). The 1926 Nature letter itself is the true primary: not opened.
- Why now: exact centenary 18 Dec 2026; fits the 1926 quantum-year wave. Expires after Dec/Jan.
- SHOW: diagrams of Lewis's "conserved photon" vs Einstein's created/destroyed quantum; page of the Nature letter (1926, public domain in US; check Nature's rights); portraits (Wikimedia, PD).
- Format: Short (strong), could be the cold open of a long film on 1926.
- Overlap: none directly; adjacent to done "quantum field theory" (different angle). Risk: low; history-of-science nuance (who used "photon" first is contested; use Lewis-letter claim only).

### 2. Mars is hot on one side: the southern interior is 200-400 C warmer than the north
- It seems: Mars is a dead, cold, uniform rock. But really: gravity data say the southern half of the interior is 200-400 C warmer and perhaps partially molten.
- Fact + source: Berne et al., Nature, published 27 Aug 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10893-x. Method: tidal tomography (seasonal tidal gravity variation) from decades of tracking of Mars Global Surveyor, Odyssey, MRO. Press: https://phys.org/news/2026-08-thermal-anomaly-mars-south-pole.html and https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/08/260828082336.htm . Read level: press release only, paper paywalled (abstract not reached). Origin unexplained: giant impact heat, convection, or heat-trapping structure (three open hypotheses).
- Why now: fresh (5 weeks). Does not expire quickly.
- SHOW: a real-run simulation of a body's tidal bulge (Love number) and a cutaway; hemispheric map from MOLA (NASA, public domain); InSight seismology images (PD).
- Format: long film (with the Mars dichotomy and InSight) or Short.
- Overlap: close to "interior of the Earth by seismology" and "Moon seismology" (in production); different planet, different method (gravity not seismic), so a companion. Risk: single paper, mechanism contested; say so on screen.

### 3. A magnetar may show empty space bending light (vacuum birefringence)
- It seems: a vacuum is nothing, so it cannot change light. But really: in a field of ~2.2e14 gauss, QED predicts the vacuum acts like a crystal; X-rays from magnetar 1E 1547.0-5408 were polarised 65% averaged, nearly 80% at some phases, and the polarisation locks to the field as the radio waves do.
- Fact + source: Stewart et al., "Vacuum birefringence and the polarized X-ray emission from a radio magnetar", Nature 656, 590-594 (2026), DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10859-z; arXiv 2509.19446 https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19446 (abstract read; full text not read); press 5 Aug 2026 https://phys.org/news/2026-08-rare-ultra-magnetic-star-key.html . Instruments IXPE, NICER, Parkes. Authors say "strongest evidence", not confirmed.
- Why now: published Nature, Aug 2026; follow-up IXPE data pending (unknown date).
- SHOW: simulation from a real run is possible only as a toy (polarisation transport in a dipole, our own code); real IXPE/NICER images (NASA, PD) and Parkes photos (CSIRO, check licence); diagram of virtual pairs.
- Format: Short or long. Overlap: done "quantum field theory" film (virtual particles) - this is the experimental payoff; check script to avoid repeating. Risk: not confirmed; alternative magnetar emission models exist (an arXiv 2601.15452 titled "the elusive smoking gun" exists: lead only).

### 4. BepiColombo arrives at Mercury on 21 Nov 2026: one burn, no second chance, 22 min round-trip signal

- Fact + source: ESA "Latest updates: BepiColombo's arrival at Mercury" https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/BepiColombo/Latest_updates_BepiColombo_s_arrival_at_Mercury (summary read): transfer module separated 3 Sep 2026 (>200 million km from Earth); orbit insertion 21 Nov 2026; MPO and Mio separate 9-10 Dec 2026; science from April 2027; thruster test 24 Sep fired 8 min 42 s, 11.4 m/s. Launch 2018. Delay from thruster fault found Sept 2024 (from search summary, lead). Also Science Daily 11 Sep: Mercury's fiery past (lead).
- Why now: dated event 21 Nov; ESA/JAXA joint. Expires after insertion (could be recut as "arrived" film).
- SHOW: orbital mechanics sim from a real run (Hohmann/gravity-assist chain with real ephemeris, our own integration); ESA images (CC BY-SA IGO 3.0 for most ESA material, check each); MESSENGER/NASA images (PD).
- Format: long film (the journey) timed to land before 21 Nov, or Short. Overlap: none. Risk: a failed burn would change the story; film must be written to survive either result, or released after.

### 9. Light sails: past ~75% c, scattered light becomes drag
- It seems: a faster light-sail always accelerates more slowly but never reverses. But really: Doppler shift cuts thrust, and above a critical speed (about 0.75 c per press) diffusely scattered light points forward and drags.
- Fact + source: Shen & Li, "Relativistic Lightsail Propulsion Dynamics", arXiv 2606.04052 (2 Jun 2026) https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04052 (abstract read: states a "critical velocity" for the diffuse term; the 75% number is from press https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/08/260831015157.htm - check in the full text). Note: paper is June; news wave late Aug.
- Why now: news wave Aug-Sep; no dated event. Preprint, not peer-reviewed as far as read.
- SHOW: our own relativistic photon-momentum calculation (it is closed-form; "from a real run" is a computed curve, label it) ; Breakthrough Starshot renders (check licence).
- Format: Short. Overlap: none (touches "GPS needs Einstein" in spirit: relativity at work). Risk: preprint, one group; says it ignores heating and ISM.

### 10. Quantum free fall: an atom's phase agrees with Einstein's equivalence principle
- It seems: gravity and quantum mechanics clash, so a falling atom must misbehave. But really: ultracold rubidium atoms in a split-path interferometer show the free-fall phase exactly as predicted; it is a consistency test of the equivalence principle in quantum mechanics, not quantum gravity.
- Fact + source: Dobkowski et al., "Observation of the quantum phase of free fall and the consistency with the equivalence principle", Science Advances, 2 Sep 2026, DOI 10.1126/sciadv.aec8045; Oxford release https://www.physics.ox.ac.uk/news/scientists-observe-einsteins-gravity-quantum-world (release read; paper not opened; no quantitative precision found). Led by Ben-Gurion, Ulm, Oxford (with Penrose, Vedral per Oxford/search).
- Why now: new; no date hook.
- SHOW: our own atom-interferometer wave simulation (Schrödinger split-step, a real run of a simple model); diagrams; Oxford photos (licence unknown).
- Format: long or Short. Overlap: sits beside "GPS needs Einstein" and "Einstein in Sobral 1919" (both in production) as the 2026 tail of the equivalence principle: a natural third part, or rejected as duplicative. Risk: press language "quantum gravity" is misleading; the correct frame is the story.
